## Section 1: Product Context

**Product Name:**
Ledgerlane

**Product Description:**
Ledgerlane automates accounts payable for mid-market finance teams. It reads invoices from email and portals, matches them to purchase orders, routes exceptions to the person who can fix them, and pays on the right date. It replaces the spreadsheet-and-inbox process that most finance teams of 5 to 15 people still run.

**Stage:**
Growth (about 400 customers, expanding from mid-market into upper mid-market)

**Target Market:**
Finance teams at companies with 200 to 2,000 employees that process 3,000 to 40,000 invoices a month and run an ERP they do not want to replace.

**Value Proposition:**
Ledgerlane helps finance teams close the month faster and with fewer invoice errors, without replacing their ERP or adding headcount.

**Offerings (what we sell, packaged):**
Two plans: Core (capture, matching, approvals) and Control (adds exception routing, audit trail and payment runs). One add-on: supplier portal.

**Approved Terminology (we say X, not Y):**
"Exception" not "issue". "Finance team" not "back office". "Payment run" not "batch".

**Permitted Claims (may be made without further approval):**
Go-live in about five weeks. Works on top of the existing ERP. [src: Ledgerlane implementation records | 2026-09-30 | stated | confirmed]

**Business Model (how you charge, what the price scales with, self-serve or sales-led):**
Annual subscription priced on invoice volume bands, with a platform fee for the Control plan. Sales-led: a 30-minute demo, then a two-week pilot on the buyer's own invoices.

200–2,000 employees, $30M–$500M revenue

**Economic Buyer:**

**Champion Profile:**
Senior AP analyst or AP manager who lives in the inbox and the spreadsheet. Wants fewer manual matches and a way to show the Controller the numbers.

**Top 3 Pain Points:**
1. Invoice errors found after payment (duplicate payments, wrong amounts) that take days to claw back.
2. Month-end close stretches past 8 working days because invoices sit in inboxes.
3. AP analysts spend most of their week keying and chasing, not analyzing.

**Buying Triggers:**
A failed audit finding on duplicate payments. A new Controller or CFO who wants a faster close. Invoice volume growing 30% or more without headcount to match. An ERP upgrade that exposes how many manual workarounds exist.

**Jobs to Be Done (When __, I want to __, so I can __):**
1. When month-end is two days away, I want every invoice matched and approved, so I can close on time.
2. When an invoice looks wrong, I want it routed to the person who can fix it, so I stop chasing people by email.

**Cost of the Problem (time, money or risk, with a number and its source):**
About 1.5 FTE of keying time per 20,000 invoices a month, and roughly one avoidable duplicate payment a quarter. (Estimate from three customer interviews; not yet confirmed with finance data.)

**Anti-Persona (who is NOT a fit, and why):**
Teams under 2,000 invoices a month (the cost of the tool outweighs the saving) and companies that plan to replace their ERP within a year (matching rules would be rebuilt).

**Buying Committee (standing roles, what each cares about, what makes each block):**

- IT or ERP owner (technical blocker): security and ERP integration. Blocks if it needs custom development.
- AP analyst (end user): fewer manual matches. Blocks if the tool is slower than the spreadsheet.

**Named Alternatives (Top 3):**
1. The ERP's built-in AP module (already paid for, but weak at exceptions and approvals)
2. A large AP suite aimed at enterprise (powerful, but a 6-month rollout and a services bill)
3. Offshore data-entry outsourcing (cheap per invoice, but slow and error-prone)

**Status Quo (If they don't buy anything):**
Invoices arrive in shared inboxes, an analyst keys them into the ERP, and the Controller reconciles in a spreadsheet. Cost of inaction: about 1.5 FTE of keying time and one avoidable duplicate payment a quarter.

**Why They Leave Alternatives:**
The ERP module cannot route exceptions to the right person. The enterprise suite takes too long and needs a consultant. Outsourcing shifts the keying but not the errors, and adds a handoff.

**Unique Capabilities:**
Exception routing to the budget owner in the tool they already use, matching that learns from the finance team's own corrections, and go-live in about five weeks on top of the existing ERP.

**Category:**
Accounts payable automation for mid-market finance teams

**Market Type:**
Existing category, resegmented: enterprise AP suites exist; Ledgerlane is built for teams without an implementation team.

**Where Each Alternative Falls Short (for this ICP):**

- Enterprise AP suite: six-month rollout and a services bill a 10-person team cannot absorb.
- Offshore data entry: moves the keying but not the errors, and adds a handoff.

**Switching Forces (push, pull, habit, anxiety):**

- Anxiety: "Will it break our ERP posting?" and "Will the team trust the matching?"

## Section 5: Market Context

**Market Maturity:**
Growing. Buyers know AP automation exists; the question is which approach fits a team without an implementation project.

**Macro Forces Making Our Solution Relevant:**
1. Finance teams are asked to close faster with flat headcount.
2. Fraud and duplicate-payment risk is getting more attention from auditors.
3. Invoice formats keep fragmenting (email, portals, PDFs), which breaks template-based capture.

**Why Now:**
Auditors are asking harder questions about payment controls, and finance leaders are being asked for faster close without hiring. Both push teams off spreadsheets this year. [src: three customer calls | 2026-08-20 | inferred | confirmed]

**Market Narrative (The bigger story):**
Finance operations is moving from keying data to handling exceptions. The teams that make that move first close faster and catch errors before payment.
