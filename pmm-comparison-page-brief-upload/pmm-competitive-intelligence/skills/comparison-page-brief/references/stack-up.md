# Stack Up

Three lenses. A page built on one lens overclaims.

## Contents
1. Head-to-head
2. Field position
3. Switching reality
4. Hypothesis check and do-not-build gate

## 1. Head-to-head

Per Field Map row: WIN, PARITY, LOSE or UNKNOWN, with confidence (high = both sides S1/S2 and fresh; medium = one side S3; low = inferred). UNKNOWN is a result, not a failure; it becomes `Not publicly available` on the page.

## 2. Field position

Read up to five roster rivals. A claim that two or more rivals make equally is crowded: it may appear in the table, but it is never the headline differentiator. Name the open position: what you can say that the field cannot.

## 3. Switching reality

From Section 3 and 6, interview notes and `_outcomes.md`: `why_leaving` (why buyers leave the rival), `switching_forces` (what holds them: data, contracts, habit, integrations), and outcomes. An outcome pattern needs n of at least 2 before it is stated; with n of 1 it is an anecdote and stays out of the page.

## 4. Hypothesis check and do-not-build gate

Treat each brain belief about the rival as a hypothesis: confirmed, contradicted or untested. Contradictions are shown to the user before anything is written. Do-not-build: if you do not win a row a target buyer cares about, say it, name the fix (product, price, a different page type) and ask whether to continue. A page that cannot be defended is worse than no page.
