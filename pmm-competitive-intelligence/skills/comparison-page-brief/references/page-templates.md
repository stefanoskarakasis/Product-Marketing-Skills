## 1. Choosing the type

`vs`: buyers compare you to one named rival. `alt-single`: searchers want an alternative to one rival (target their dissatisfied users). `alt-list`: searchers want a ranked field. `switch`: existing users of the rival need migration help. Search intent for each: "[you] vs [rival]", "[rival] alternative", "best [category] tools", "migrate from [rival]". Do not combine types on one page.

## 2. VS

Verdict line above the fold, then: compare at a glance table; deep dives by category; who should choose which; pricing with units; switching block; proof; FAQ; one CTA. The verdict names a winner per buyer type, not a universal winner.

## 3. ALT-SINGLE

Compact: why people look for an alternative (from `why_leaving`, sourced), what to look for, how you answer each, honest cases where the rival is still better, switching block, CTA.

## 4. ALT-LIST

Default 7 entries, max 10, identical entry template: one-line fit, best for, pros and cons (each sourced), pricing (annual-vs-annual), rating with source and date. Your own entry uses the same template, same depth, and includes a con. Order by buyer fit, and say how entries were chosen.

## 5. SWITCH

What moves (data, users, integrations), what does not, a sample import run first, time and effort estimate only if sourced, undo path, and the keep-your-current-tool (coexist) option. Name every limitation.

## 6. Shared blocks and keywords

Switch block, FAQ (real buyer questions from Section 3 and sales notes), CTA (one). `keyword-targets.md`: primary and secondary terms with intent labels; volume only from a pasted export, never estimated. `schema.json`: FAQPage and WebPage facts that exist on the page; no rating schema without a real rating. `page.md`: plain-text twin of the page for retrieval and LLM answers.
