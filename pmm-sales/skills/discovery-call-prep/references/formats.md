# Formats

Read at Step 8. Pick the surface first; never ask the seller which format they want.

## Slack (about 150 words)
The reply is the card only: no run notes, assumptions or owner questions below it. Assumptions go on the Sources line.

Hard cap 150 words including the closing question; count before sending, and cut the least useful line first. Layout: a bold title line, then a bold header with one emoji for every section (Slack has no `#` headers). Under each header, bullets only, one sentence per bullet, each an action or a testable statement (Ask, Say, Listen, Do), never a description. The fallback must be a different pain from the lead, not its opposite. Before sending, check every Ask bullet opens How, What or To what extent (never Who, Where, Why, Do, Is); rewrite any that do. No tables. Roles only. Nothing quoted from calls. Nothing after the closing question. One bullet under Ask names which later questions to use if time allows. Missing context (no approved story, no sourced edge) is one clause under Sources. In a channel with people outside the company, stop and say so. Ends on a plain question, never on keyword replies.

```
**📞 [Company] · [role] · [N] min · [first / follow-up / open deal]**

**🎯 The bet**
• Lead: [hypothesis, one sentence].
• Confidence: [label] · [level].
• Fallback: [a different pain].

**🚪 Open**
• Ask: [opener question]
• Say: [framing line, one sentence].

**❓ Ask, in order**
• [Stage 1 question]
• [Stage 2 question]
• [Stage 3 or 4 question]

**👂 Listen for**
• Confirms: [one thing].
• Kills: [one thing].

**💥 If it goes sideways**
• Trigger: [one line].
• Move: [one line].

**🏁 Fast no**
• Say: [line].

**📎 Sources**
• [Two, with dates; Learned from: skills or none yet].

How did the call go: priority confirmed, changed, or not a fit?
```

If the seller asks for the full plan, offer it as a Doc or in chat; do not paste the seven stages into a thread.

## Slack short form (open deal, about 120 words, hard cap 150)

Same rules as above. Labels in this order, each one line, the questions as bullets:

```
🎯 **Objective:** [one line]
🔍 **Gap to close:** [the evidence marked not found or stated in the last record, in plain words]
❓ **Ask, in order:**
• [question 1]
• [question 2]
• [question 3]
⚠️ **Likeliest objection:** [one line, the card or Section 3 entry] → [the move]
✅ **A good call changes:** [what moves from stated to validated]
📎 **Sources:** [record and date] · Learned from: [skills, or none yet]

How did the call go: did the gap close?
```

Never a verdict, never the seven stages.

## Full plan (chat, Cowork, Claude Code or a Doc)

Real headers, sized differently from body text. Bold, not italics. Emoji only for a section, action or status. Every line carries its source and date in plain words. Empty sections never print. A plan the seller returns to is delivered as a Doc if `~~cloud storage` is connected (ask before saving, naming the folder), and saved to `/context/discovery/`.

0. **Call card.** First thing in the plan: the Slack form above, at most 150 words, so the seller can glance at it during the call. Everything below is the working detail.
1. **Snapshot.** Company, role and family, call type and minutes, entry state, the aim in two to four sentences (what they are optimising for, the likely tension, the sharpest angle), the dreaded email as a hypothesis about the role.
2. **Point of view.** Lead hypothesis and fallback first, each with signal, source, date, label, confidence and the question that would kill it. Then the three company and three team areas as one line each, with the validating question. Stale signals marked.
3. **Opening.** The opener, the framing line, the validation questions.
4. **Seven-stage plan.** For each stage run for this length of call: going-in hypothesis, ready questions with their ids, what good looks like, the red flag. Stages not earned: one question each, marked "if earned".
5. **Where we are different, in their words.** Only for sourced edges: the capability as the buyer would say it, the question that gets them there, the competitor it is aimed at. If none sourced: one line naming the gap and the coaching: name one capability you own that a known alternative cannot match, then work backwards to the buyer's words. Status quo is the competitor when none is named.
6. **Pitfalls on this call.** Two or three, concrete.
7. **If the point of view misses.** The fallback hypothesis and the fast-disqualify line.
8. **Notes as you go.** Confirmed priorities in the buyer's words, process details to dig into, challenges to revisit. A recorder catches the rest.
9. **Paste-ready blocks.** A CRM note for the planned call, the three questions for Slack, and the one-line log reply.
10. **Not queried and learned from.** Which connectors answered, which did not, and which sibling ledgers shaped the plan.

## Short form (open deal)

As `references/modes.md`: objective, gap, three questions, likeliest objection, what a good call changes. Under 150 words in Slack.

## Language

Direct, short sentences, no filler openers. Warm and honest: name the hard part, then the move. Coach, never grade. Avoid "leverage", "crush it", "let me be clear", hype and over-formatting. Non-English accounts: write the questions in the buyer's language only when the seller asks; otherwise give them in English with a note.
