# Method

Read before every run. Stage detail is in `stages.md`.

## 1. Stance

- Grade the seller's discovery skill, not the deal. A shallow call coached as solid helps nobody; every honest mark comes with a way up.
- A first call earns the right to deeper discovery. It is not graded on reaching all seven stages. A stage that was not this call's job is Not reached yet, and the coaching names what the next call is for.
- Discovery is multi-call. Cramming everything into one call is a miss of its own.
- Name the pattern across the call, not only each stage: where the seller is strong, where it came off, and the one habit that would change the most.

## 2. Orient before grading

| Ask | Why |
|---|---|
| Which call is this | A fourth call stuck in current state differs from a first call that is |
| Who is the buyer | Executive metrics put to a contributor lose them; workflow minutiae put to a VP waste the VP |
| How many stages could this call honestly reach | The bar is the discovery that fits this call, plus whether it set up the next one |

If the call number or title is not given, infer it from the transcript and say what you assumed.

## 3. Status and trajectory

| Mark | Means |
|---|---|
| ✅ Strong | Clear evidence, done as great discovery looks |
| ⚠️ Developing | Present but shallow or partial |
| ❌ Needs Work | Missing when it should be there, or it hurt the call |
| ⬜ Not reached yet | Not this call's job. No penalty |

Trajectory ↑ improving, → holding, ↓ slipping, only against an earlier review of the same seller; otherwise "single call". Needs Work means they were here and it was weak, or should have been and were not. Not reached yet means it was not this call's job. Get that difference right every time.

## 4. Technique, graded once across the call

- **Exchange or extraction.** Look for "the reason I ask is" plus an insight or a story. All questions then all monologue is an interrogation: the buyer worked and got nothing back. A good seller leaves the buyer having learned something even if they never buy.
- **Buyer bias.** Buyers arrive guilty until proven innocent ("they do not know my business", "they do not listen", "they will push"). Did the seller clear it early or feed it?
- **Engagement.** Did the seller read low engagement and say so ("Feels like I might be missing the mark; is this close to something you have seen?") instead of plowing on, and treat a correction as engagement instead of defending?
- **Set-up of the next call.** Right depth for this call, an agreed dated next step and a second thread; not all of discovery crammed into one call.

## 5. Name the pattern

| Pattern | Reads as |
|---|---|
| Credibility Gap | Generic view, bias-validating openers; never earned the right to ask |
| Too Broad, Too Early | Many problems, no priority |
| Surface-Level Pain | Current state captured, no "so what" (the most common) |
| Skipped the Priority Test | Deep discovery on a problem never shown to be a funded priority |
| Weak Value Translation | After state reached, no outcome the buyer can fund |
| Strong Start, Weak Finish | Good view and priority, shallow consequences and outcomes |
| Interrogator | Coverage without exchange |

The pattern is the headline. Pick one or two. A strong call may fit none: say so, and name the habit to protect instead of inventing a flaw.

## 6. Replay: find the moments

Two to four, ordered by cost. A moment is one of:

- **An open door walked past.** The buyer named a pain, number, person or deadline and the seller acknowledged it and moved on. The most valuable kind.
- **A question that cost credibility.** Bias-validating, unearned confidence, "that makes sense" when it did not.
- **A turn.** Engagement dropped, the buyer corrected the seller, or asked for a demo or price to escape discovery. What the seller did next set the rest of the call.
- **Time on the wrong thing.** Minutes on an unvalidated problem or on breadth when depth was available.

Type each fix; the type decides the coaching:

| Type | Coach as |
|---|---|
| Line | A different question or reply in the moment. Write the words. Every question you write, in a replay, a coaching line or a move, opens How, What or To what extent |
| Prep | Decided before the call: a view too generic, the buyer's metrics unknown, no story ready. Coach the preparation and where it comes from, not a smarter line |
| Allocation | Nothing to say would fix it; where the time went did. Name what to spend less on and what the time buys |

State what the move opens, not what it would have delivered: often it shows whether something is there. Do not armchair-quarterback; skip the other twenty questions.

## 7. Plan versus call

With a plan for the account: was the lead hypothesis tested (confirmed, changed, not asked), which planned question ids were asked, which unlocked evidence the plan lacked. This is the join that sharpens the next plan; it adds no mark.

## 8. Coach and next-call moves

Coach each Developing or Needs Work stage, most important first: what happened (the transcript moment), why it matters, what tends to follow if it stays. Do not coach Not reached yet as a failure; name it as what the next call is for. Then two to four moves, each the habit named plainly plus an "In practice" line the seller can say verbatim, drawn from `stages.md`. Where a move came from a replay moment, say so in a few words. If the deal itself needs work, close with one line pointing to `deal-review`.

## 9. Several calls

Score the trajectory: is the view getting sharper, is the "so what" landing more often. Use only the seller's own `mine/` files, or the transcripts they pasted together. Say which calls were read.
