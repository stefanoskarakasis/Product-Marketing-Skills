# Price play

Read when the objection is about cost, fee, budget, discount or "too expensive". A price objection is rarely about price. Diagnose the type first.

## The seven types

| Type | Signal | Real issue | Lead move | Wrong response |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Premature | Raised on call one or two, before value is sized | Price seen before pain | Park it; size the problem | Defending or discounting |
| 2 Budget fixed | "We have [X] allocated" | A ceiling, or a number set before the problem was sized | Locate: ceiling or starting point? Ask what the last comparable purchase cost | Cutting scope unasked |
| 3 Cheaper alternative | A rival with a lower price | Unlike scopes compared | Normalise scope; rival claims to `competitive-battlecard` | Quoting a rival's price |
| 4 Status quo | "We do it in-house or free today" | Hidden cost of the current way | Size the status quo | Attacking the process |
| 5 Value unclear | "I don't see the return" | The value was never made concrete | Size, then prove | Repeating features |
| 6 Reflex tactic | Said at the table, no reasoning | A negotiation opening | Trade table; never concede in the first reply | An immediate discount |
| 7 Structure | "Annual upfront is hard", "per seat doesn't scale" | Cash flow or model fit | Offer structure: term, ramp, cadence | Treating it as a price cut |

## Role variants

| Who | Needs | Adjust |
|---|---|---|
| C-level or economic buyer | Outcome and risk in a minute | One number, one risk, skip mechanics |
| VP or champion | Ammunition for the approver | Build the approver kit below |
| Procurement | Terms, comparability, a defensible file | Trade table and total cost; do not sell value to someone who cannot buy |
| Finance | Payback and total cost | Cost of problem, payback, cash timing |
| End user | They do not own price | Ask who owns the budget; do not negotiate |

## Lines (slots only; tailor in `_library.md`)

- **Park it:** "Fair question, and I don't want to quote you a number before we know what this is worth to you. Can we spend five minutes on what [problem] costs today, then come back to price?"
- **Isolate:** "Setting the number aside: if the price were right, is there anything else that would stop this?"
- **Locate:** "When you say [budget], is that a ceiling, or the amount set before this problem was sized?"
- **Re-anchor:** "You told me [problem] costs about [their figure] per [period]. This is [price] per [period]. Does that comparison hold on your side?"
- **Status quo:** "You said it's handled in-house. Who handles it, how many hours, and what happens when [failure case] happens?"
- **Trade:** "I can't move on price alone. If [commitment], I can look at [lever]. Which is realistic for you?"
- **Approver:** "Who signs this off, and what do they care about most? I'd like to give you something you can forward."

## Approver kit

One page the champion can forward: the problem in the buyer's numbers, the price against it, the alternative they are comparing, the risk of doing nothing, and the one decision needed. Sourced lines only.

## Trade table

| Give (from you) | Get (from them) |
|---|---|
| Longer or shorter term, a ramp, a cadence change | A longer commitment, volume, an earlier start |
| A pilot or phased scope | A named success measure and a decision date |
| A reference or case study right | A reference, case study or intro to the approver |

Levers must be on the approved list in Section 8 `approvals`. Anything else: route to the approver and say so.

## Don'ts

No discount in the first response. No "you get what you pay for". No rival's price from memory. No invented ROI. No false urgency. No floor revealed. No concession on a call without the approver. No attack on the alternative. No tax or legal advice: confirm VAT and invoicing entity with Finance.

## Renewal variant

An increase is a different conversation. Anchor on what was delivered (usage, outcomes) before the number. Ask what changed on their side. Offer structure before price. Never open with the increase.

## EMEA variant

Longer cycles, heavier procurement, sometimes a formal tender or works-council step. Hard-close tactics land badly. Lead with a defensible file. Confirm currency, VAT and invoicing entity early. Name the multi-stage sign-off in the next step.

## When the numbers say no

If the buyer's own figures make the fee higher than the saving, say so, stop arguing price, and offer the honest options: a smaller scope, a different structure, or parking it until the volume changes. A deal you cannot make work for them is a deal to release.

## Reads, never invents

`cost_of_problem`, `approved_metrics`, `sales_cycle`, `committee_roles`, `alternatives`, `anti_persona`. A missing number becomes one question.
