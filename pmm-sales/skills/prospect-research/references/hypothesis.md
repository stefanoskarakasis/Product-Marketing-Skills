# Hypothesis, Angle and First Question

Read at Step 5. The brief exists to produce these six lines. Everything above them is evidence.

## 1. The six lines

| Line | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Hypothesis** | One sentence: *because [signal], [account] is likely facing [pain from Section 2], which lands on [the function or process]*. Labelled `inference`. Names one to three signals. Carries a confidence |
| **Angle** | What to open on, chosen by the seniority of the person being contacted (section 2). Starts from the account's situation, never from your product |
| **First question** | One question, answerable in a sentence, that tests the hypothesis. It confirms or kills the pain; it does not ask for a meeting |
| **Fallback** | What to do or ask if the answer is no: the next most likely pain, or "park it". Not a second pitch |
| **Warmer** | A similar customer, from approved proof in Section 6 only. If none is approved, write "none approved". Never use an unapproved metric or name |
| **What they probably believe** | The current approach or status quo the buyer likely holds, from Section 3 `status_quo`; if the brain has none, "not in the brain". Buyers arrive partly informed and sometimes wrong: this is the assumption to respect before testing it |

## 2. Angle by seniority

| Contact | Open on | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Director and above | A company priority or event they have said or announced in public | Anything about the person themselves beyond their role |
| Manager or practitioner | A problem their role owns or is measured on | Company strategy they do not control |
| Unknown role | The function and the process, not a person | Guessing a name or a title |

Use a personal detail only when it is professional, public and tied to the pain (a talk they gave on the topic). A school, a hometown or praise for their "journey" is decoration and is dropped.

## 3. Confidence

| Level | When |
|---|---|
| High | Two or more independent signals map to the same pain, and one is the account's own words |
| Medium | One signal maps to the pain, or two signals are both third-party |
| Low | One weak or ageing signal, or the brief was built without a brain |

Never write high for a hypothesis built on one signal. A low-confidence hypothesis is still shown; it is labelled and its first question carries more weight than its angle.

## 4. First-question rules

- Specific to the signal: it names the thing (a rollout, a hire, a tool), not a category.
- Neutral: it does not assume the pain exists.
- Free to answer: no data request, no demo, no time ask.
- No personal detail the account has not published.
- In EMEA mode, no reference to anything inferred about a person.

## 5. What this step never does

- Draft the outreach, a subject line or a sequence. If asked, offer a three-line opener built from these six lines and hand off to `writing-assistant` for tone.
- Present a hypothesis as a finding, or a competitor claim as fact.
- Stack more than three signals behind one hypothesis. If more exist, write a second hypothesis and say which is stronger.
