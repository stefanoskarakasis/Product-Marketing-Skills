# Demo Script, Talk Track and Deck Outline

Read for a demo script, talk track or deck outline. A demo before discovery is a guess about which features matter. If the ask shows no discovery has happened, say so in one line, point to `discovery-call-prep`, and still draft with the gaps marked.

## Types

| Type | Minutes | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| First demo | 30 to 45 | Three or four workflows tied to the buyer's named pain |
| Technical deep dive | 45 to 60 | Architecture, security, integrations |
| Executive overview | 20 to 30 | Outcome, cost of the problem, next step |

## Scenes (sized to the minutes given)

1. **Open (2 min).** Confirm the goal of the call and the agenda in one line each.
2. **Discovery recap (3 min).** Say back the priority in their words and ask whether it still holds.
3. **Walkthrough.** Three or four workflows, each opening with their pain, then the screen, then "what does this change for you?".
4. **Interaction points.** A question inside every workflow, not just at the end.
5. **Close (5 min).** Summarise the value in their terms, propose one next step with a date.

## Rules

- Their terms and their use case; their data if the rep has it.
- Leave at least a fifth of the time for the buyer to talk.
- Every claim on screen follows `claims.md`. A feature the product owner has not confirmed is marked "confirm before showing".
- Cold call openers and voicemails go to `writing-assistant`.

## Deck outline

Output is an outline with a headline, the point and speaker notes per slide. The visual build is a design or Slides job (the design brief is in `one-pager.md`).

## Story arc (default, not a template)

Take the arc from the brain, not a fixed twelve slides. A typical order:

1. The problem the buyer lives with, in their words.
2. What it costs them (Section 2 `cost_of_problem`, or their own number).
3. The shift that makes it urgent now, only if a dated source supports it.
4. The approach, one idea.
5. Three or four workflows tied to their pain.
6. Proof from Section 6, dated.
7. One approved customer story, or a named gap.
8. How they get from here to live.
9. The value in their numbers.
10. Pricing, only from approved pricing.
11. The next step, with a date.

## Match the buyer

| Buyer | Lead with | Leave out |
|---|---|---|
| Technical | Architecture, security, integrations | ROI maths |
| Economic | Cost, payback, risk | Implementation detail |
| Champion | What they can repeat to their boss | Deep technical or financial detail |

## Rules

One idea per slide. Design for presenting, not reading. Every claim follows `claims.md`. Marked "needs approval before sending".
