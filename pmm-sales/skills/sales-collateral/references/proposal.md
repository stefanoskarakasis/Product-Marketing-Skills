# Proposal

Read for a proposal. It follows discovery; it does not replace it. Aim for five to seven pages. Past ten, nobody reads it.

## Sections

1. **Executive summary (one page).** Their challenge in their words, what you will deliver, the outcome they said they want.
2. **Proposed solution.** Mapped to the requirements they named, one by one. A requirement you cannot meet is stated, not hidden.
3. **Implementation plan.** Milestones, who does what, what you need from them.
4. **Investment.** Price, terms and what is included, from approved pricing only. No approved price: leave a marked blank for the rep, never a number.
5. **Next steps.** The decision date and the one action to move.

## Customisation

- Reuse the buyer's language from calls and notes. Cite what they said, with the date.
- Name the people you have met by role; in EMEA mode, roles only.
- Include only the proof that matches their industry or use case, from Section 6.
- Mark every assumption as an assumption.

## Mistakes to avoid

Too long. Too generic. A buried price. A promise the product owner has not confirmed. A claim about a rival. Urgency the buyer did not date.
