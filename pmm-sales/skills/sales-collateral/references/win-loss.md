# Win-Loss Analysis

Read for `winloss`. It turns outcomes, calls and buyer interviews into a sourced answer to "why do we win and lose, and against whom?". It is the evidence the battlecard, objection and discovery skills need, and it feeds them as proposals only.

## Steps

1. **Inventory sources and their bias.** CRM loss reasons (rep-entered, skewed to price and timing), call transcripts (what the buyer said in the deal), buyer interviews (most candid, smallest sample). Say which were read and which were not queried.
2. **Include wins.** A loss reason means little without the deals won against the same rival.
3. **Segment before you count.** Split by rival, segment, deal size and stage lost. "We lose on price" across everything often hides "we lose mid-market to one rival on onboarding".
4. **Code each deal.** One primary reason, any secondary ones, a supporting quote and its source. One coder, or a double-coded sample, so "primary" means the same throughout.
5. **Report counts with n.** "6 of 9 losses to Acme cite onboarding time". Under 5 deals is a signal to watch, not a finding. A deal with only a one-word CRM reason is low confidence until a transcript or interview confirms it.
6. **Turn findings into proposals.** To the owning skill, with the exact text and the counts. Never a silent edit.

## Reason codes (starting set, the PMM edits it)

Product fit · Price or packaging · Missing proof · Switching cost · Incumbent or status quo · Timing or no decision · Security or legal · Champion lost · Process (slow, no next step) · Other (named).

## Buyer interview questions (for the owner to run)

1. What triggered the search?
2. Who was involved and who decided?
3. Which options did you consider, and how did you narrow them?
4. What mattered most in the end, and what nearly changed your mind?
5. What did we do well, and where did we fall short?
6. What would have changed the outcome?

## Report

Scope and sources; findings with counts and quotes; by-rival breakdown; asset updates proposed; what was not read; confidence.

## Rules

By deal, segment and rival only. No seller names, no ranking of people. Recordings are read only after the owner has decided per source (`connectors.md`). A rival's name is shown only where a source names it.
