## H-003: Behavioral Messaging Review intake (Step 1) can be skipped for warm audiences
- **Status:** testing
- **Hypothesis:** When audience type = D (existing customer) and the copy format is
  email, the three-question intake in Step 1 adds friction without adding useful
  signal. The context is already sufficient to proceed.
- **Evidence for:** Two sessions where users with D-type audiences skipped intake
  answers and asked to proceed directly to the rewrite.
- **Evidence against:** None yet
- **Signal:** Weak — need 3+ more data points before confirming
- **Next test:** Next Mode 3 session with a D-type audience. Offer to skip intake
  and note whether the output quality held without it.
- **Current instruction impact:** SKILL.md Step 1 already has an exception for this.
  Watching to confirm it's correct.

## H-004: Specificity in social proof increases copy credibility more than volume claims
- **Status:** testing
- **Hypothesis:** "Used by the growth team at [Company X]" outperforms "used by 500+
  companies" in contexts where the reader is evaluating a tool for their own team.
  Volume claims feel generic; named proof feels like a signal about fit.
- **Evidence for:** Theoretical — grounded in Harhut's specificity-as-credibility
  principle, not yet observed across real sessions.
- **Evidence against:** None
- **Signal:** Theoretical only
- **Next test:** First Mode 3 session reviewing SaaS homepage or outbound copy —
  compare response when specificity vs. volume social proof is recommended.
