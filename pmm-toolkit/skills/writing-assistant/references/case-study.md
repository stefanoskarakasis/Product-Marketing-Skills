# Case study

Customer proof. This is the format where invented results do the most damage.

## Gate

Without real results (numbers, or specific outcomes the customer confirmed) and the
customer's permission to publish, write a skeleton with placeholders and label it at the
top: "Not publishable until the items below are filled." Never fill a gap with a
plausible figure.

## Inputs beyond the shared set

- Customer name, or an anonymous descriptor, plus industry and size.
- The situation before, what they tried first, what changed.
- Measured results with a baseline and a timeframe.
- Permission status (named, anonymous, or not yet asked) and a quote.

Default length: 500 to 800 words.

## Structure

1. **Title.** The customer and the result in one line.
2. **Snapshot.** Customer, industry, size, product used, the headline result.
3. **Challenge.** What was going wrong, in the customer's terms.
4. **Solution.** What was set up, by whom, and how long it took.
5. **Results.** Real numbers, each with its baseline and timeframe ("from X to Y in Z
   weeks"). A placeholder where a number is missing.
6. **Quote.** `[QUOTE: customer name and title, the point they made]`, unless the user
   supplied the exact words.
7. **CTA.** One next step for a reader who looks like this customer.

## Rules

- The customer is the hero. The product is the tool they used.
- Use the customer's own words for the problem where the user has them.
- If the customer is anonymous, stay anonymous. No hints that identify them.
- Before publishing, the customer approves the final copy. Say so in the notes.
