# Landing page

Covers landing pages, homepages, and product pages. One page, one job.

## Inputs beyond the shared set

- The offer and the single primary CTA.
- The main alternative the visitor would pick instead: a competitor, a spreadsheet, or
  doing nothing. Brain Section 3 holds it. If the brain doesn't, ask.
- The top two or three objections that stop a visitor from acting.

Default length: as long as the objections need, usually 300 to 600 words of visible copy.

## Structure

1. **Hero.** Headline, subheadline, primary CTA. Offer three headline options, each from
   a different angle: the outcome, the pain removed, the comparison with the alternative.
2. **Proof near the top.** A customer logo, a real stat, or a quote, taken from brain
   Section 6. If none exists, a placeholder that says what is needed.
3. **Value props.** Three or four. Each leads with the benefit, then says how it works.
4. **How it works.** Three steps at most.
5. **Objections.** A short FAQ or trust signals, built from the objections in the brief.
6. **Final CTA.** The same action as the hero. Add a risk reducer only if it is true.
7. **SEO.** Title tag, meta description, URL slug. See `seo-checklist.md`.

## Rules

- One primary CTA. A quiet second link is fine. A second competing button is not.
- Benefit before feature. Speak to the reader as "you".
- The headline says who it is for, what it does, and why it beats the alternative, in
  about ten words or fewer. The subheadline adds something and never repeats it.
- Every section answers "so what?" from the reader's side.
- Competitor test: could a competitor paste this headline about their own product? If
  yes, rewrite it.
- Cut jargon unless the audience uses it themselves.
- No em dashes anywhere on the page.
