# Press release

Use a press release only when there is news.

## News gate (run this first)

Ask: what is new, and who outside the company would care?

- **Counts as news:** a new product or major feature, funding, a partnership or
  acquisition, an executive hire, a milestone with a real number, a customer win the
  customer has approved, an event.
- **Does not count:** an opinion, a minor update, restated positioning.

If there is no news, say so in one sentence and offer a blog post or an announcement
post instead. Do not manufacture newsworthiness.

## Inputs beyond the shared set

- The news in one sentence, the date, and any embargo.
- The city for the dateline.
- Who is quoted (name and title) and whether they have approved wording.
- The boilerplate: brain Section 1, or a placeholder. The media contact.

Default length: 400 to 500 words.

## Structure

1. **Release line.** "FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE", or "FOR RELEASE ON [date]" when there is an
   embargo.
2. **Headline.** States the news plainly, with the company name, in one line. No hype.
3. **Subheadline (optional).** Adds one fact.
4. **Dateline and lead.** CITY, date, then the lead paragraph: who, what, when, where,
   and why it matters, in one to three sentences. The lead must work alone if the rest
   is cut.
5. **Body.** Supporting detail in order of importance. Add context, plus availability
   and pricing if relevant.
6. **Quote.** One or two. Write `[QUOTE: speaker name and title, the point they should
   make]`. Write the actual words only when the user supplied them exactly.
7. **Boilerplate.** From brain Section 1, or `[PLACEHOLDER: company boilerplate]`.
8. **Media contact,** then `###` to close.

## Rules

- Third person. Facts in plain tense. No marketing adjectives.
- Every number comes from the user or the brain, or it is a placeholder.
- Never attribute invented words to a named person. This is the highest-risk failure in
  this format.
